## 9.2 Metrics and the Ops Agent

= dia | Metric prefixes | The prefix tells you where a metric comes from
p9-hypervisor
N: Metrics are identified by type, and the prefix tells you where they come from. Compute dot googleapis comes from the hypervisor: C-P-U, disk and network input and output, with no agent needed. Agent dot googleapis comes from the Ops Agent: memory, swap, disk space used and processes. Workload dot googleapis is application metrics through the agent's receivers. And custom, logging user and prometheus prefixes are custom metrics you write, log-based metrics you define, and Managed Prometheus.
V: The hypervisor cannot see inside the guest. Memory appears only after the Ops Agent is installed.
[pause: 0.8]

= list | The Ops Agent | One agent, replacing two
- Combines **Fluent Bit** (logs) and an **OpenTelemetry collector** (metrics)
- Configured in `/etc/google-cloud-ops-agent/config.yaml`
- **Integrations** for nginx, MySQL, PostgreSQL, JVM, Redis and more
- The VM's service account needs **`logging.logWriter`** and **`monitoring.metricWriter`**
- On a fleet: an **OS policy** through VM Manager, not a script by hand
N: The Ops Agent combines Fluent Bit for logs and an OpenTelemetry collector for metrics, in one agent, replacing the legacy Logging and Monitoring agents. It has integrations for common applications. Its V-M's service account needs Log Writer and Metric Writer. So an installed agent that sends nothing often lacks those roles. And to install on a fleet, use an O-S policy through V-M Manager, not a script run by hand on each V-M.

## 9.3 Alerting, uptime checks and SLOs

= dia | One idea | Only one of these notifies anyone
p9-alert-only
V: Only an alerting policy notifies anyone.
[pause: 0.8]
N: Dashboards, uptime checks, log-based metrics and S-L-Os all measure. An alerting policy decides and sends. So when an option gives you a dashboard, or an uptime check alone, for a requirement to page someone, it is incomplete.

= table | An alerting policy | The parts
Part | Meaning
**Condition** | Threshold · **absence** · **forecast** · **PromQL** · **log match**
**Duration** | How long it must hold: *"for 5 minutes"* avoids flapping
**Notification channels** | Email, SMS, mobile, Slack, PagerDuty, webhooks, **Pub/Sub**
Documentation | Markdown with the notification: the runbook link
Incident | Opens when it fires; closes automatically when it clears
N: The parts of a policy. The condition can be a metric threshold, metric absence, a forecast, a PromQL query, or a log match. The duration says how long it must hold, so for five minutes avoids flapping. Notification channels are email, S-M-S, mobile app, Slack, PagerDuty, webhooks and Pub/Sub, defined once and reused. Documentation is sent with the notification, usually the runbook link. And an incident opens when the condition fires, closing automatically when it clears.

= table sm | Requirement to construct | Six requirements
Requirement | Construct
Page when p95 latency > 800 ms for 5 minutes | **Alerting policy**, threshold, 5-min duration
Public endpoint unreachable from several regions | **Uptime check + alerting policy on it**
Notify **each time** a specific error line appears | **Log-based alert**
**More than 50 times in 10 minutes** | **Log-based metric (counter) + metric alert**
Error budget burning too fast | **SLO + burn-rate** alert
A metric **stops arriving** | **Metric-absence** condition
N: Six requirements, and the construct for each. Page when latency stays high for five minutes: an alerting policy with a threshold and a duration. Tell us if the public endpoint is unreachable from several regions: an uptime check, plus an alerting policy on it. Notify each time a specific error line appears: a log-based alert. Alert when a line appears more than fifty times in ten minutes: a log-based metric and a metric alerting policy. Alert when the error budget burns too fast: an S-L-O with a burn-rate policy. And a metric that stops arriving: a metric-absence condition.

= panel watch | Easy to confuse | Log-based alert versus log-based metric
A **log-based alert** notifies on individual matching entries, with a rate limit: *"tell us whenever X is logged"*.
A **log-based metric** turns entries into a **time series**: rates and thresholds, charts, SLOs.
Error Reporting groups exceptions, but cannot express *"more than 50 in 10 minutes of this plain line"*.
N: Keep these two apart. A log-based alert notifies on individual matching entries, with a notification rate limit. It is the simplest answer to, tell us whenever this is logged. A log-based metric turns matching entries into a time series, needed for rates and thresholds, charts and S-L-Os. And Error Reporting groups exceptions, but it cannot express more than fifty in ten minutes of a plain line.

= cards c2 | Uptime checks and SLOs | Two more things that measure
Uptime checks :: Probe HTTP(S), TCP or ICMP from several locations, or privately inside a VPC via **Service Directory**. They alert only through an attached policy.
grn SLOs :: On a **service**, with an **SLI**, a target and a window. The **error budget** is the allowed failure. A **burn-rate** alert fires when it is consumed too fast.
N: Uptime checks probe H-T-T-P, T-C-P or I-C-M-P endpoints from several Google locations, or privately inside a V-P-C, and they chart availability and latency. They alert only through an attached policy. S-L-Os are defined on a service, with an S-L-I, a target and a window. The error budget is the allowed failure, and a burn-rate alert fires when the budget is being consumed too fast. That pages on fast budget consumption, and not on every blip.

## 9.5 Cloud Logging: routing, buckets, Log Analytics

= dia | The Log Router | Every entry passes through sinks to a destination
p9-router
N: Follow the flow. Log entries reach the Log Router, which evaluates sinks, each with inclusion and exclusion filters. Each sink has a destination. The underscore Required bucket has four hundred days of fixed, free retention. The underscore Default bucket has thirty days by default. A custom bucket lets you choose your region, retention, and encryption key. A BigQuery dataset is for constant querying and joining. Cloud Storage is cheap long-term archive. A Pub/Sub topic streams out to a S-I-E-M, Splunk or on-premises. And another project is for central logging.

= table | Log buckets | What goes where
Bucket | Retention | Holds
**`_Required`** | **400 days, fixed, free** | **Admin Activity** and **System Event** audit logs
**`_Default`** | **30 days** default, configurable | Everything else not excluded, including **Data Access**
**Custom** | 1–3,650 days, can be **locked** | Anything a sink routes there
> **Exclusion filters** drop entries **before storage**: the lever for the bill. Routing is free. **Log views** limit who sees what inside a bucket.
N: Three kinds of bucket. Required holds Admin Activity and System Event audit logs, for four hundred days, fixed and free. Default holds everything else not excluded, including Data Access logs, for thirty days by default, and that is configurable. Custom buckets hold anything a sink routes there, and can be locked. Exclusion filters drop entries before storage, and they are the lever for the logging bill, so we do not need debug logs from development is an exclusion filter. Log views restrict who sees which logs inside a bucket.

= dia | Aggregated sinks | Central logging, and the classic failure
p9-sink
N: An aggregated sink on a folder or organization, with include children, routes every project's logs beneath it. That is the answer to, central logging for all projects. Follow the arrows. One, logs arrive. Two, the sink writes as its writer identity, a service account. In the first frame, with no grant at the destination, the write is denied, and the sink silently does nothing. In the second, with the role granted at the destination, entries land.
V: Each sink's writer identity must be granted write access at the destination.
[pause: 0.6]
N: That role is BigQuery data editor, Storage object creator, Pub/Sub publisher, or log bucket writer, depending on the destination.

= panel key | Log Analytics | SQL over logs, without a second pipeline
Upgrading a log bucket lets you query logs with **SQL**, and optionally create a **linked BigQuery dataset** that queries the same data **from BigQuery without copying it**.
*"Analysts want SQL over our logs, and to join them with business data, without a second pipeline."*
N: Upgrading a log bucket to Log Analytics lets you query logs with S-Q-L, and optionally create a linked BigQuery dataset, which queries the same data from BigQuery without copying it. So analysts want S-Q-L over logs, and to join them with business data, without a second pipeline, is Log Analytics with a linked dataset.

= cmd sm | Logging commands | Buckets, sinks, identity, exclusions
Task | Command
Central bucket, Log Analytics | gcloud logging buckets create central --location=europe-west4 --retention-days=365 --enable-analytics
Every project's logs into it | gcloud logging sinks create all-to-central logging.googleapis.com/projects/LOGS_P/locations/europe-west4/buckets/central --organization=ORG_ID --include-children
Who to grant at the destination | gcloud logging sinks describe all-to-central (writerIdentity)
Stop paying for debug logs | gcloud logging sinks update _Default --add-exclusion=name=no-debug,filter='severity<INFO'
Keep `_Default` longer | gcloud logging buckets update _Default --location=global --retention-days=90
N: Here are the commands in order. Create a central bucket, with analytics enabled. Create an organization sink with include children. Describe the sink to find its writer identity, which you then grant at the destination. Add an exclusion to the default sink, to drop debug logs. And update the default bucket to keep logs longer.

= table | Exporting to external systems | Destination by requirement
Destination | Right when
**Pub/Sub** | Stream to a **SIEM**, Splunk, or an **on-premises** collector
**BigQuery** | Query constantly, join, dashboard
**Cloud Storage** | **Retain cheaply for years**, read rarely, with lifecycle to Archive
A log bucket, long retention | Keep logs in Logging's own tools and Log Analytics
N: Export by requirement. Pub/Sub for streaming to a S-I-E-M, Splunk, or an on-premises collector. BigQuery for constant querying and joining. Cloud Storage to retain cheaply for years, with a lifecycle rule to Archive. And a log bucket with long retention to keep logs in Logging's own tools.

## 9.6 Reading logs

= code | Logs Explorer | The Logging query language
resource.type="gce_instance" AND severity>=ERROR
resource.type="cloud_run_revision" AND resource.labels.service_name="api"
logName="projects/P/logs/cloudaudit.googleapis.com%2Factivity"
protoPayload.methodName="v1.compute.firewalls.patch"
protoPayload.authenticationInfo.principalEmail="mara@kestrel.io"
jsonPayload.orderId="A-1042"    textPayload:"timeout"    httpRequest.status>=500
timestamp>="2026-09-20T14:00:00Z" AND timestamp<"2026-09-20T15:00:00Z"
> `=` equals · `:` has or contains · `=~` regular expression · `AND`, `OR`, `NOT`
N: Logs Explorer and gcloud logging read use the Logging query language. Read these as examples. Filter by resource type and severity. Filter by a service name label. Filter by log name, here the activity audit log. Filter by method name, or by who called it, in authentication info, principal email. Filter by payload fields. And filter by time. Equals means equals, a colon means has or contains, and equals tilde is a regular expression.

= table | A log entry's fields | What "view log message details" means
Field | Holds
`logName`, `resource` | Which log; type and labels: which VM, service, cluster
`timestamp`, `severity`, `insertId` | When, how bad, a unique ID
`trace`, `spanId` | The link to Cloud Trace
`httpRequest` | Method, status, latency
Payload | `textPayload`, `jsonPayload`, or **`protoPayload` for audit logs**
> `gcloud logging read 'resource.type="k8s_container" AND severity>=ERROR' --freshness=1h`
N: A log entry's fields. Log name and resource tell you which log, and which V-M, service or cluster. Timestamp, severity and insert I-D. Trace and span I-D link to Cloud Trace. H-T-T-P request holds method, status and latency. And there is one payload: text, JSON, or proto payload for audit logs, with method name, service name, resource name, and authentication info.

## 9.7 Audit, flow and firewall logs

= dia | Four audit log types | The defaults decide most items
p9-audit
N: Four audit log types. Admin Activity records calls that change configuration or metadata. It is always on, cannot be disabled, and is free. Data Access records calls that read configuration, or read or write user data. It is off by default, except for BigQuery, and it is charged. System Event records Google-initiated changes, such as live migration, always on and free. And Policy Denied records requests denied by a security policy.
V: Who changed it is Admin Activity. Who read it is Data Access, which exists only if it was enabled before the reads.
[pause: 0.8]
N: So we have no record of who downloaded the file usually means Data Access logging was never turned on.

= list | Enabling Data Access logs | Part of the IAM policy
- `auditConfigs` in the IAM policy, at the organization, folder or project
- Per service, per type: `ADMIN_READ`, `DATA_READ`, `DATA_WRITE`
- Optional **exempted principals**, such as a noisy batch account
- Console: **IAM & Admin → Audit Logs**
- Reading them needs **`roles/logging.privateLogViewer`**
> Flow logs and firewall logs are not audit logs, but read the same way, and charged by volume. Tune flow-log sampling.
N: Enabling Data Access logs is part of the I-A-M policy, in audit configs, at the organization, folder or project. It is per service and per type: admin read, data read, data write. You can exempt principals, such as a noisy batch service account. Reading these logs needs the private log viewer role. V-P-C Flow Logs and Firewall Rules Logging are not audit logs, but the guide groups them here, because they are configured and read the same way. Both are charged by volume, so tune flow log sampling and aggregation, and log only the rules you need.

## 9.8 Diagnosing an application

= dia | Which diagnostic tool | Which, why, what changed
p9-diagnose

The database is slow: Query Insights on Cloud SQL and AlloyDB, which shows top queries, load and plans, and index advisor, which recommends indexes from the real workload. New objectives, both of them.

## Part 9 sample questions

= th | Part 9 · practice | Nine exam-style questions | What measures, what notifies, and what had to be enabled beforehand.
N: Nine questions. Ask what measures, what notifies, and what had to be switched on beforehand.

= q | A dashboard shows CPU for a fleet of VMs, but memory utilization is missing. What should you do?
(A) Enable the Cloud Monitoring API
(B) Install the Ops Agent, with `logWriter` and `metricWriter` on the VM's service account
(C) Add a metrics scope
(D) Move the VMs to a larger machine type
ans B
why A :: CPU appears, so the API is already working.
why B :: The hypervisor cannot see memory inside the guest.
why C :: Scopes view other projects; they do not create new metrics.
why D :: Changes nothing about what is measured.
C: The answer is B. The hypervisor cannot see inside the guest, so memory appears only after the Ops Agent is installed, and the V-M's account needs the two writer roles. A cannot be right, because C-P-U charts already work. C and D do not produce a new metric.

= q | On-call must be paged if the public checkout endpoint becomes unreachable from several regions. What is the minimum complete setup?
(A) An uptime check
(B) A dashboard showing latency
(C) An uptime check, plus an alerting policy on it
(D) A log-based metric of errors
ans C
why A :: Measures, but it notifies nobody.
why B :: Someone has to be watching it.
why C :: The check measures; the policy notifies.
why D :: A site that is unreachable may log nothing.
C: The answer is C. Only an alerting policy notifies anyone. A is the near miss, because an uptime check measures but sends nothing. D has a subtle flaw: if the service cannot be reached, it may log nothing at all.

= q | A specific error line must trigger an alert when it appears **more than 50 times in 10 minutes**. What should you create?
(A) A log-based alert
(B) A log-based counter metric, and an alerting policy on it
(C) Error Reporting
(D) A sink to BigQuery, with a scheduled query
ans B
why A :: Notifies on individual entries, not rates.
why B :: Turns the entries into a time series for a threshold.
why C :: Groups exceptions; it cannot express this threshold.
why D :: Assembled, and slower.
C: The answer is B. More than fifty in ten minutes is a rate, so you need a time series, meaning a log-based metric with a metric alert. If the stem had said notify each time, the answer would be a log-based alert. D could work, but it is assembled, and we prefer managed.

= q | You must route logs from all 200 projects to a central bucket, including future projects. You create a sink, but nothing arrives. Which **two** are required? Choose two.
(A) An aggregated sink at the organization or folder, with `--include-children`
(B) Grant the sink's writer identity write access at the destination
(C) Enable Data Access logs on every project
(D) Create one sink per project
(E) Set `_Required` retention to a longer value
ans A, B
why A :: Covers every project beneath, including new ones.
why B :: Without this, the sink writes nothing.
why C :: Unrelated to routing.
why D :: Misses future projects.
why E :: That retention is fixed, and unrelated.
C: The answers are A and B. Including future projects points to an aggregated sink. Nothing arrives points to the writer identity lacking a grant at the destination, the classic failure. D misses future projects. E is impossible, since the Required bucket's retention is fixed.

= q | After an investigation, a security team finds no record of who downloaded certain objects from a bucket last month. What is the most likely reason?
(A) Admin Activity logs were disabled
(B) Data Access logs for Cloud Storage were not enabled before the reads
(C) The `_Required` bucket has expired
(D) VPC Flow Logs were sampled too low
ans B
why A :: These cannot be disabled, and they cover changes, not reads.
why B :: Off by default, and not retroactive.
why C :: 400 days, and it does not hold reads.
why D :: Flow logs do not identify users or objects.
C: The answer is B. Reads are Data Access logs, which are off by default, apart from BigQuery, and they are not retroactive. A is impossible, because Admin Activity cannot be disabled. C is the wrong bucket. D records network flows, not who read an object.

= q | Logging costs are high because a development project writes huge volumes of debug logs that nobody reads. What should you do?
(A) Add an exclusion filter on the `_Default` sink, for severity below INFO
(B) Lower `_Required` retention
(C) Create a budget for Cloud Logging
(D) Disable Admin Activity logs in that project
ans A
why A :: Drops entries before storage, so they are not charged.
why B :: Fixed, free, and not where the cost is.
why C :: A budget only alerts.
why D :: Cannot be disabled, and they are free.
C: The answer is A. Exclusion filters drop entries before storage, which is the lever for the logging bill. B and D are impossible, and also aimed at free logs. C alerts, and reduces nothing.

= q | Latency jumped for a request that crosses four microservices. CPU utilization looks normal on all of them. Which tool first?

(B) Cloud Trace
